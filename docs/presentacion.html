Phonendo on Stellar

The insurer knows the rehab session was completed and pays for it. It never sees the pulse.

Phonendo collects sensor readings, checks that they are authentic and generates a zero-knowledge proof that they meet the doctor's plan. A Soroban contract verifies that proof with Stellar's native cryptographic functions and pays the patient in USDC in the same transaction.

01 · Problem and users

Paying for adherence without asking for health data

After a heart attack, cardiac rehabilitation reduces readmissions, but many patients drop out. It pays off for an insurer to give a small reward for each completed session: a readmission costs far more.

The obstacle is privacy. Checking that the session was done properly means looking at the pulse, and health data is a special category under the GDPR. The insurer should not receive it and the patient should not have to hand it over.

A session counts if the pulse stayed in the zone set by the doctor, for example 90 to 130 beats per minute, in at least 4 of 5 readings. Phonendo proves exactly that and nothing more.

Pays: the insurer

Deposits USDC in the contract and sets the policy. Its panel shows “session completed”, the amount and the transaction on stellar.expert.

Gets paid: the patient

Receives the payment in her Stellar account automatically. Her readings stay in Phonendo and only she sees them in the panel.

Earns: Phonendo

Charges a fee for each verified proof. One deployment serves several policies because minimum, maximum and count are public parameters.

Same circuit, another case

Cold chain: a buyer pays if the shipment stayed within the temperature range. Only the policy values change.

02 · Architecture

From the wristband to the payment

  1. The sensor measures and the Reader publishes

    Each reading goes out as an observation with readerId and deviceId. The Reader has Python and C++ cores and examples for Linux and Arduino; in the demo it simulates a pulse of 95 to 120 bpm.

  2. The Manager hashes the event and asks for verification

    It adds an HMAC-SHA256 managerHash of the content and sends it to the Verifier, over HTTP or SSE, retrying on timeout.

  3. The Verifier checks and signs

    It validates fields, dates and hash, signs the event with ed25519 and remembers the reading. Only Verified events reach Storage.

  4. The Publisher builds the batch and generates the proof

    It listens to the Manager's stream, collects 5 readings from one device, derives the sessionId from their event ids, picks a random salt and runs the Groth16 circuit. If the batch does not meet the policy, there is no proof and no payment.

  5. The Verifier signs the batch, not the Publisher

    The Publisher only sends the ids of the 5 events. The Verifier checks that the sessionId comes from those ids and that no event was already signed in another session, recomputes the commitment from the readings it verified itself and signs commitment ‖ sessionId ‖ patient account ‖ verifierId. So whoever gets paid cannot make up the data.

  6. claim() verifies and pays in one transaction

    The contract rejects repeated sessions, checks the ed25519 signature and the proof against the policy stored on-chain, marks the session, transfers the payment to the patient and emits SessionPaid.

  7. The panels read the payments from the chain

    The patient, cold-chain and company panels read the SessionPaid events from the testnet RPC and link each transaction on stellar.expert. None of them receives health readings.

03 · Privacy

What leaves Phonendo and what does not

Stays off-chain

  • The 5 pulse readings of each session
  • The batch's random salt
  • Verified events and their signatures, in Storage
  • Device and Reader identity
  • Logs and internal state of the services

Published on Stellar

  • The commitment: a Poseidon fingerprint of the batch that does not reveal the readings
  • The public policy: 90, 130 and 4 of 5
  • The sessionId and the patient's G… account
  • The Groth16 proof and the Verifier's signature
  • The payment and the SessionPaid event

Without the salt, the commitment does not let anyone guess the readings even though they only range from 0 to 255. And because the sessionId goes into the proof and the signature, a valid proof cannot be reused to claim another session. The same readings always give the same sessionId, so they are never paid twice, even with several Publishers.

04 · Fit with Stellar

One call with native functions

All verification happens inside claim(), with Soroban host functions. There is no off-chain verifier and no intermediate oracle.

05 · The code

Piece by piece

ZK circuit

circuits/ · Circom 2 · Groth16 on BLS12-381new at the event

range_adherence.circom takes the 5 readings and the salt as private signals, and the policy and the sessionId as public ones. It counts how many readings are in range with 8-bit comparators and requires the count to reach minCount. If it does not, no proof can be generated.

At the same time it computes the commitment with lib/commitment.circom: it packs up to 30 readings per field element (always two elements) and applies Poseidon together with the session and the salt. The public signal order is [commitment, minValue, maxValue, minCount, sessionId] and the contract uses the same one. It is compiled with -p bls12381 because Circom uses BN254 by default.

range_adherence.circom · count and condition 
for (var i = 0; i < N; i++) {
    commit.readings[i] <== readings[i];

    ge[i] = GreaterEqThan(8);
    ge[i].in[0] <== readings[i];
    ge[i].in[1] <== minValue;
    le[i] = LessEqThan(8);
    le[i].in[0] <== readings[i];
    le[i].in[1] <== maxValue;

    inRange[i] <== ge[i].out * le[i].out;
    count[i + 1] <== count[i] + inRange[i];
}

component enough = GreaterEqThan(COUNT_BITS);
enough.in[0] <== count[N];
enough.in[1] <== minCount;
enough.out === 1;                 // without this there is no proof

commitment <== commit.out;

component main {public [minValue, maxValue, minCount, sessionId]} = RangeAdherence(5);

Soroban contract adherence

contracts/adherence/ · Rust · soroban-sdknew at the event

The constructor stores the Verifier's public key, the payment token, the policy and the circuit's verification key. claim() trusts nothing the caller sends: the policy limits come from the contract's storage and the payment goes to the account the Verifier signed.

Anyone can submit the claim, usually the Publisher. Only the admin can change the policy with set_policy.

src/lib.rs · claim() 
pub fn claim(env: Env, patient: Address, session_id: u64, commitment: BytesN<32>,
             signature: BytesN<64>, proof: Proof) -> Result<(), Error> {
    // 1. Anti-replay
    if env.storage().persistent().has(&DataKey::Claimed(session_id)) {
        return Err(Error::AlreadyClaimed);
    }
    // 2. Verifier signature over commitment ‖ sessionId ‖ patient key ‖ verifierId
    let config = Self::config(&env);
    let mut message = signed_message(&env, &commitment, session_id, &patient)?;
    message.append(&Bytes::from_array(&env, &config.verifier_key.to_array()));
    env.crypto().ed25519_verify(&config.verifier_key, &message, &signature);

    // 3. Groth16 proof with the stored policy, never the caller's
    let policy = &config.policy;
    inputs.push_back(Bls12381Fr::from_bytes(commitment));
    inputs.push_back(fr(&env, policy.min_value as u128));
    inputs.push_back(fr(&env, policy.max_value as u128));
    inputs.push_back(fr(&env, policy.min_count as u128));
    inputs.push_back(fr(&env, session_id as u128));
    if !verify_groth16(&env, &verification_key, &proof, &inputs) {
        return Err(Error::InvalidProof);
    }

    // 4. Mark, pay and announce
    env.storage().persistent().set(&claimed, &true);
    token::Client::new(&env, &config.token)
        .transfer(&env.current_contract_address(), &patient, &policy.payout);
    SessionPaid { session_id, patient, amount: policy.payout }.publish(&env);
    Ok(())
}

Verifier: batch signature

apps/verifier/ · Python · Flasked25519 new at the event

This is the piece that binds the proof to real data. The Verifier keeps every reading it verifies. When POST /signBatch arrives, it only accepts 5 distinct events that it verified, that belong to one device and that were not signed in another session. It recomputes the commitment with its own Poseidon implementation, identical to the circuit's, and signs it. The Publisher checks that it matches the one in its proof before claiming.

Publisher

apps/publisher/ · Pythonnew at the event

It listens to the Manager's /stream and groups 5 readings per device, counting each event only once even if the stream repeats it. It generates the proof with the circuits/ scripts, asks for the signature with signBatch and sends claim() with the Stellar CLI. A batch that does not meet the policy is logged and not claimed.

publisher.py · process_batch() 
def process_batch(batch):
    """Proves the batch, gets it signed and claims the session with claim()."""
    try:
        commitment, proof_file = prover.prove(batch)
        signature = get_signer().sign(batch, commitment, batch['patient'])
        tx_hash = claimer.claim(batch, batch['patient'], commitment, signature, proof_file)
        Logger.info('publisher_session_paid', sessionId=session_id, txHash=tx_hash)
        return tx_hash
    except BatchNotMet:
        Logger.info('publisher_session_not_met', sessionId=session_id)

Data pipeline

apps/reader · manager · storage · Python and C++

Reader, Manager, Verifier and Storage make up the data pipeline. It was adapted at the event: the Reader simulates a pulse, the Manager avoids redundant HTTP/SSE connections per service, the Verifier signs with ed25519 instead of RSA and every service exposes /info and /logs for the panel.

08 · Quality

The tests behind it

  • Contract: pays a valid session only once and rejects a proof for another policy, a proof for another session, a signature for another patient, a signature without the Verifier's identity and a tampered proof.
  • Circuit: inputs/session.json and cold_chain.json produce a proof; session_fail.json, with out-of-range readings, cannot.
  • Python services: about 150 component and integration tests, including two Verifiers with two Publishers, with real processes, ed25519 signatures, SSE streams and a temporary LevelDB; the Docker stack is tested with all five services.
  • Web: tests of the canvas logic with Node and of the panels and console with Vitest: states, connections, logs, RPC payments and container deployment.
09 · Limits

What it does not solve yet

  • We trust the Phonendo Verifier and the sensor. The signature guarantees that the readings are the ones the Verifier checked, not that the sensor measured correctly.
  • The circuit key ceremony is done by a single participant: fine for development, not for production.
  • Poseidon uses circomlib's constants over the BLS12-381 field. It is tested against the circuit, but these are not standard parameters and do not match Stellar's native Poseidon function.
  • The circuit checks the value of each reading and how many are in range, but not the time between readings: 5 readings taken within a few seconds count as a session.
  • The Publisher keeps batches in memory: if it stops, it loses the batch in progress and does not retry claims.
  • The contract is on testnet and has not been audited. The operator console only works locally: its API listens on loopback only.

11 · Market and roadmap

Why now and what comes next

Customer

Health insurers and rehabilitation programmes that want to reward adherence without handling clinical data.

Revenue

A fee for each verified proof that ends in a payment.

Why now

Soroban verifies Groth16 proofs on BLS12-381 natively, and USDC on Stellar makes it possible to pay in the same transaction.

Why Phonendo

It already had a tested pipeline that captures, verifies and stores sensor data; Stellar adds proof and payment.

  1. Signing on the device

    The ESP32 signs each reading with P-256 so there is no dependence on a single Verifier.

  2. Several Verifiers and a multi-party ceremony

    Spread the trust and generate the circuit keys among several participants.

  3. Fingerprint of all data on-chain

    Anchor the hash of the verified data, with the encrypted data in Storage.

  4. Consent in the contract

    The patient grants or revokes access to her data and the contract checks it before Storage hands it over.
